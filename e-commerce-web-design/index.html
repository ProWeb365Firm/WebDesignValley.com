E-Commerce Platform Selection

WooCommerce vs. Custom Build vs. Shopify

We build on all three. The right platform depends on your catalog, goals, and budget structure. Compare their features to help you pick one, or we’ll recommend one during a free consultation.

WooCommerce

Best for content- heavy stores

  • Runs on WordPress SEO powerhouse
  • Unlimited products and categories
  • Thousands of plugins and extensions
  • Full code ownership and portability
  • Best long-term cost of ownership

Consider: Requires hosting management; more setup complexity

Custom Build

Best for unique requirements

  • Built exactly to your spec, no limits
  • Unique UX not possible with off-the-shelf
  • No third-party platform dependencies
  • Best performance at scale
  • Full ownership of every line of code

Consider: Higher initial investment; longer build timeline

Shopify

Best for streamlined retail

  • Hosted and managed for low maintenance
  • Built-in payment processing
  • Fast store setup and onboarding
  • Excellent inventory management tools
  • Large app marketplace

Consider: Monthly fees + transaction costs; less SEO flexibility

Not sure which platform is right for you? That’s what our free discovery conversation is for. We’ll recommend the best fit for your specific products, goals, and budget. Schedule a free consultation today.

Frequently Asked Questions

E-Commerce Web Design Questions Answered

Common questions from Rochester, MN, businesses before starting an e-commerce project.

1. What product information or catalog data should I have ready before we start building my store?

Most projects move faster when clients come prepared with product photos, descriptions, pricing, SKUs, and category groupings already organized in a spreadsheet. Businesses without this data prepared in advance can still launch, but the catalog-loading phase of the build will take longer.

2. Can my new store integrate with the inventory or accounting software I already use?

Custom and platform-based stores alike can typically connect to inventory management systems, point-of-sale software, and accounting tools like QuickBooks through available integrations or APIs. Specifics available depend on the platform selected (WooCommerce, Shopify, or custom build), so this is worth confirming during the discovery conversation.

3. How do you handle product returns, refunds, and exchanges on the back end?

Return and refund logic is configured through store policies and, where needed, plugins or apps that automate refund processing and restock inventory. Clear return policies are also treated as a trust signal on the storefront itself, helping reduce cart abandonment among first-time shoppers.

4. Will my store work internationally, including multiple currencies or languages?

Multi-currency and multi-language support is possible on all three platforms but varies in complexity: Shopify and WooCommerce offer plugin-based solutions, while custom builds can be engineered for it directly. This is important to flag early if you plan to sell across multiple countries/areas.

5. How many products can my store realistically handle before performance suffers?

Catalog size limits depend more on hosting infrastructure and platform configuration than on a hard product cap, meaning a well-optimized store can scale from a few dozen products to several thousand without slowdown. Site search and filtering, in particular, need to be configured properly as catalogs grow larger.

6. What happens if I want to sell on multiple channels, like Amazon, Instagram, or a marketplace, in addition to my own site

Multi-channel selling is handled through platform-native integrations (such as Shopify’s channel apps) or third-party syncing tools that keep inventory and orders aligned across your website and other sales channels. This prevents overselling and keeps stock counts accurate everywhere your products are listed.

7. How do you protect customer data and comply with privacy regulations?

Every store includes SSL encryption and PCI-compliant checkout, and additional privacy safeguards, like cookie consent banners and data-handling policies, can be built in to align with regulations such as GDPR or state-level privacy laws. This is especially relevant for stores selling to customers outside Minnesota or the United States.
